01 · The field

Spatial audio you can see

The field is a metre-accurate map of the space around your head. Drag a sound to move it on the ground plane, hold Alt to lift it, and switch to the tilted 3D view to see height directly.

  • Binaural rendering. Each source runs through a head-related transfer function panner, plus modelled shoulder reflection and outer-ear filtering that shift with elevation.
  • Posture. Standing, lying on your back, lying on your side. The listener orientation and the anatomy filters change together.
  • One camera, two views. The 2D map and the 3D orbit are the same scene blended between orthographic and perspective, so nothing jumps when you switch.
  • Audio-reactive nodes. Each source has its own analyser. The halo and ripples follow the actual signal, not a random timer.
  • Motion paths. Orbit, ping-pong, drift and breathe run without a single keyframe, and show their path on the map.
  • Other outputs. Stereo speakers, 5.1, or a custom speaker layout you place by dragging.
  • Head tracking. On a phone, device orientation rotates the listener so the field stays put when you turn your head.

02 · The timeline

Sessions with a shape

A journey is a set of tracks over a fixed length. Every sound gets a clip and, if you want, a path. Named sections mark the phases so a forty-five minute session reads at a glance.

  • Clips. Drag to move, drag the edges to trim. A source fades in when its clip opens and out when it closes.
  • Keyframes. Drag sideways to change the time, up and down to change the level. The map draws the resulting path.
  • Mute and solo per track, so you can hear one layer against the bed while you build.
  • Snapping that follows the zoom: half a second when you are close in, thirty when you are looking at the whole hour.
  • Undo everywhere. Moves, adds, deletes, clip edits, keyframe edits, parameter changes and motion changes all sit on one stack.
  • Nine journeys built in, from a twenty-five minute deep-work block to an hour-long sound bath and a thirty minute sleep descent that ends in silence.

03 · Focus view

One screen when that is all you need

Not every session needs a map. The focus view is a single player: pick a mode, pick a length, press the ring. The readout shows exactly what is playing, in hertz. Sessions have a settling phase, a main phase and a wind-down, and a calming session tapers its own frequencies on the way out instead of just going quiet.

05 · Frequency tools

Labelled for what the research actually says

Plenty of apps sell frequencies with claims the literature does not support. This one ships the tools and tells you where the evidence stands, on the card and in the inspector.

ToolWhat it doesLabel
Pink and white noiseBroadband masking. Pink rolls off the top end, so it sits behind work instead of over it.Some evidence
Brown noiseDeeper still, and popular, but no trial covers this colour specifically. Treat it as masking and texture.Weak evidence
Breath pacerA swell you breathe with, default six breaths a minute.Some evidence
Binaural beatsA different frequency in each ear; the beat is the difference. Needs headphones and stays locked to your head.Weak evidence
Isochronic and monaural beatsA pulsed or summed tone that works on speakers too.Weak evidence
Solfeggio tones, 432 Hz, 7.83 HzPopular frequencies, offered because people ask for them.No evidence
40 Hz gammaClinical work on 40 Hz uses light and sound together, daily, with diagnosed patients. That does not transfer to listening here, and the app says so on the card.Weak evidence

Each label carries a one-line reason, not just a verdict. Pick a binaural beat and the inspector points out that it needs headphones, and offers to swap it for an isochronic pulse that works on speakers. Full references live in the repository under docs/research.

06 · Under the hood

Plain JavaScript, no framework

Audio

Web Audio API. HRTF panner per source, shoulder and pinna peaking filters, low-pass and high-pass inserts, tremolo, a shared convolution reverb bus and a master limiter.

Binaural

Beats bypass the panner and merge straight to left and right, because spatialising them would destroy the beat. The app says so in the inspector.

Rendering

Canvas 2D with a real camera: yaw, pitch, and a blend between orthographic and perspective. Picking and dragging run through the inverse projection, so they stay exact in every view.

State

A command stack for undo, scenes in local storage, and shareable links that carry the whole journey in the URL.

Build

Vite. Two pages: this one and the app. Deployed to Vercel's edge.

Tests

Vitest with jsdom across the audio engine, generators, camera, timeline, presets, scenes and the undo stack.
